# Low-Waste Gift Wrapping: Ideas That Look Good and Get Reused

Gift wrap has one of the shortest lives of anything in the house. It is bought, folded around a present, admired for a few seconds and torn off, and a lot of it can't go in the recycling afterwards. The holidays, birthdays and weddings all add up to a steady stream of paper, ribbon and tape that exists only to be thrown away.

Low-waste wrapping doesn't mean giving up on presents that look special. It means choosing wrapping that either gets used again or can genuinely be recycled or composted once it's done.

## Start With What You Already Have

### Save and Reuse Paper, Bags and Boxes
The lowest-waste wrapping is wrapping that already exists. Smooth out paper from presents you receive and fold it flat, keep gift bags and tissue paper in good condition, and hang on to sturdy boxes. A drawer or bin set aside for "wrapping to reuse" turns this from a good intention into a habit.

### Use Paper From Around the House
Plain brown paper from packaging, old maps, sheet music, newspaper, comics pages and children's drawings all make distinctive wrapping. Brown paper in particular looks deliberate with a simple tie and a sprig of greenery, and it's usually easy to recycle afterwards.

### Make the Container Part of the Gift
A jar, a tin, a basket, a mixing bowl or a tote bag can hold the present and stay useful long after it's opened. This works especially well for food gifts, toiletries and anything made up of several small items.

## Wrap With Fabric

### Try Furoshiki-Style Wrapping
Furoshiki is a traditional Japanese technique for wrapping and carrying things in a square of cloth. A few simple knots hold the fabric in place without any tape, and the cloth can be untied and used again for the next present, or for something else entirely.

You don't need special fabric to start. A scarf, a tea towel, a pillowcase or a large napkin works. Square pieces are easiest: place the gift in the middle, bring two opposite corners together and tie them, then repeat with the other two corners.

### Wrap Gifts in Something Useful
A fabric wrap that is also part of the present doubles its value. For a cook, a gift wrapped in a tea towel and tied with kitchen string is two presents in one.

## Ties, Tape and Tags

### Skip the Plastic Ribbon
Curling ribbon and synthetic bows are hard to reuse and can't usually be recycled. Cotton string, twine, wool yarn and fabric ribbon can all be saved and used again, and natural fibers can be composted once they're worn out.

### Use Less Tape (or None)
Plastic tape left on paper can cause trouble in recycling, and it often tears the paper when it comes off, which makes reuse harder. Folding paper neatly and tying it with string can hold it closed without tape at all. If you do need tape, paper-based tape is an option, and using only a few short pieces makes the paper easier to reuse.

### Make Tags From Old Cards
Last year's greeting cards can be cut into gift tags: trim the picture side to shape, punch a hole and thread through some string. Writing straight onto the wrapping paper or a fabric tie works too.

## Know What Can Be Recycled

Not all gift wrap is paper all the way through. Wrapping with foil, a metallic or plastic coating, or glitter usually can't be recycled. A common rule of thumb is the scrunch test: scrunch the paper into a ball, and if it springs back open it probably has a plastic coating, while paper that stays scrunched is more likely to be recyclable. Recycling rules vary between local authorities, so check what yours accepts, and remove tape, ribbons and bows before putting paper in the recycling.

Plain, uncoated paper and cardboard that can't be reused may also be suitable for a home compost heap, torn into small pieces. Our [Ultimate Guide to Composting](/blogs/ultimate-guide-to-composting) covers what to add and how to keep a heap balanced.

## Give Gifts That Need Less Wrapping

Some gifts need no wrapping at all. Experiences, a homemade meal, an offer of your time, or a [gift card](/gift-cards) sent by email skip the paper entirely. When you do give a physical gift, choosing something durable and genuinely wanted keeps it out of the bin, which matters more than the wrapping around it.

If you're looking for gifts that help someone else cut waste, the [zero-waste bathroom swaps](/blogs/zero-waste-bathroom-swaps) post is a good source of ideas, our [bundles](/bundles) group reusable swaps into ready-made sets, and the [Zero Waste guides](/guides-and-tips/zero-waste) go further on reducing waste around the home.

## The Bottom Line

Wrap with what you already have before buying anything new: saved paper, boxes, bags and fabric. Swap plastic ribbon and tape for string and simple folds, check the recycling rules for anything you can't reuse, and consider gifts that need no wrapping at all. Every wrap that gets used again is one less roll of paper bought just to be torn off.
